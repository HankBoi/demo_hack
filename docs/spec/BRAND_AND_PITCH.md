# Brand and Pitch Direction

## Working product name

**Tender Evidence Checker** is a clear working label for the hackathon. It describes the product without implying that a brand, trademark, or domain has been cleared. Do not spend build time on domain-dependent features.

Before public launch, check the exact name and close variants across domains, company registries, app stores, and relevant trademark databases. The team can choose a shorter global brand later; do not claim a name is available without checking.

## Positioning

**For small suppliers preparing public-tender bids, Tender Evidence Checker turns a tender pack and the supplier's own documents into a source-linked checklist that a person can review before submission.**

### Tagline

**Every requirement. Its source. Your evidence.**

### Short description

Upload a tender pack and supporting documents. Get a page-cited checklist, possible evidence matches, and clearly marked gaps to review.

### Differentiating promise

The product does not merely summarize a tender. It connects each extracted requirement to the original page and quote, then links possible supplier evidence to its file and page. Unsupported matches stay uncertain; a person makes the decision.

The local Azerbaijani-language workflow is a differentiation hypothesis, not yet proven. Validate it through supplier interviews and the event demo dataset. Existing tender AI products mean the team should not claim to be the first or only tender AI tool.

## Visual and verbal direction

- Tone: precise, calm, practical, trustworthy.
- Visual cue: a document page with a highlighted source line connected to a checklist row.
- Avoid: gavels, shields, “AI guarantees compliance,” green all-clear scores, or imagery suggesting official government endorsement.
- Status colors must not be the sole indicator; pair each with readable text and an icon/label.
- Use “possible match,” “not found in uploaded files,” “needs review,” and “source quote verified.” Avoid “compliant,” “eligible,” or “safe to submit.”

## Pitch copy

### One sentence

Small suppliers can miss tender requirements while searching across long PDFs and company folders. Tender Evidence Checker creates a page-cited checklist and suggests where their own documents may support each item, leaving final review with the supplier.

### 30-second pitch

“A small supplier can receive a long tender pack and still has to manually turn it into a checklist, then find the right certificates and supporting files. Tender Evidence Checker reads one tender pack, extracts candidate requirements with exact page references, and suggests possible matches from the supplier's documents. It also marks missing, expired-looking, or uncertain items for a person to review. We do not decide legal eligibility or submit the bid; we make the checking work visible and easier to verify.”

### Two-minute demo sequence

1. Show the fictional supplier and public or fictional tender pack; state the manual task.
2. Upload the pack and show the extracted requirement with its original page and exact quote.
3. Show one evidence candidate linked to a supplier file/page.
4. Show one absent, expired-looking, ambiguous, or unreadable item and explain why it needs human review.
5. Correct or confirm one row, then export the checklist.
6. Show actual measured quality and manual comparison result, including sample size. If not measured, say so plainly.

## Claims to avoid

- “Guarantees compliance,” “finds every requirement,” “prevents rejection,” or “wins more tenders.”
- “100% accurate,” “hallucination-free,” or a time/cost-saving percentage without reproducible measurements.
- “Official,” “government approved,” or “integrated with etender.gov.az.”
- “The supplier lacks this document” when the system only searched the uploaded pack.
- “The document is expired” when the date or reference date is ambiguous.

## Brand validation before wider use

Interview 3–5 small suppliers or procurement consultants if feasible. Check whether the name is understood, whether the workflow is genuinely painful, what existing tools they use, which languages/documents dominate, and whether the source-linked checklist changes their work. Record who was interviewed only with consent and keep personal details out of the demo dataset.
